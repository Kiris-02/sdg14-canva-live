TARGET 14.1 QUESTION 1 • TARGET AIM

TARGET 14.1: REDUCE MARINE POLLUTION

By 2025, prevent and significantly reduce marine pollution of all kinds, in particular from land-based activities, including marine debris and nutrient pollution.

✥ Kéo
Land-Based Contaminants
Harmful chemical, physical, or biological materials that originate on land and pollute soil, groundwater.
✥ Kéo
Target aim
Stop land-based contaminants—which account for roughly 80% of marine pollution—from reaching the ocean
✥ Kéo
2026
- The target has not been fully achieved.
- Continued efforts and stronger policies are made to drive further progress

TARGET 14.3 QUESTION 1 • TARGET AIM SPEAKER 5

Target 14.3: 
Minimize and address the impacts of ocean acidification, including through enhanced scientific cooperation at all level

✥ Kéo
PLANETARY SINK
25% CO2 Absorbed
Oceans absorb a quarter of anthropogenic carbon emissions, creating carbonic acid (H2CO3).
✥ Kéo
ACIDITY SURGE
+30% Acidity
A 0.1 unit drop in global surface seawater pH represents a 30% surge in hydrogen ion (H+) concentration.
✥ Kéo
CALCIFIER CRISIS
Corrosive Waters
Depletion of carbonate ions (CO3²⁻) dissolves shells of pteropods, shellfish, and coral skeletons.
The ocean acts as a climate shield, absorbing ~25% of human CO2 emissions.
Acidification increased by 30% (a 0.1 drop on the logarithmic pH scale).
Depletes carbonate ions required for shell and skeleton formation.

TARGET 14.3 QUESTION 3 • MEASUREMENT METHODS

Measurement Method 14.3:

Autonomous Biogeochemical Profiling Across the Global Water Column

✥ Kéo
Primary Indicator (14.3.1):
Tracks average marine pH at representative monitoring stations.
✥ Kéo
Scientific Basis:
Measures pH decline caused by the ocean absorbing ~30% of human CO₂ emissions, reducing carbonate ions needed by shell-forming marine life.
✥ Kéo
Historical Trends:
Ocean acidity increased ~26–30% since the Industrial Revolution. Surface pH dropped from ~8.15 (1950) to 8.05 (2020), now at ~8.1.
